## INTRODUCTION

Local anaesthetic agent lidocaineprilocaine cream is widely being using in various clinical settings. It is effective in relieving the pain of venous and arterial cannulation in child as well as in adults.<sup>1,2</sup> It was also found that it is alleviated the pain in lumbar puncture, epidural injections, drug reservoir injections and other procedures involving needle insertion.<sup>3,4</sup> In minor surgical procedures such as circumcision, skin grafting and excision of small dermal masses lidocaineprilocaine cream was found to be effective in pain relief without any major complications.<sup>5,6</sup> Although its popular use in various surgical procedures there is not adequate knowledge about its effects on wound healing. Therefore we designed an experimental study to evaluate the effects of lidocaine prilocaine cream on wound healing in experimental conditions.

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

Forty adult male inbred mice from Animal Laboratory Unit of SDU were used in this study. The animal ethics committee of the faculty has approved and controlled the study. All of the guiding principles in the use and care of laboratory animals were strictly adhered throughout the entire study. ## DISCUSSION

Lidocaineprilocaine cream is a safe, effective topical anaesthetic for both child and adult population. But, it may cause methaemoglobinaemia in infants.<sup>8</sup> Two of its metabolites 4-hydroxy-2-methylaniline and 2- methylaniline have been implicated in this condition. Although the maximum methaemoglobin levels Ž . median 2.24% were not clinically significant, caution is advised in this age group. Skin reactions, such as pallor, erythema, alterations in temperature sensation, oedema, itching and rash are common following lidocaineprilocaine application, but generally are mild and transient.<sup>9</sup>

In surgical procedures, especially in dermatological surgery and in skin grafting lidocaineprilocaine cream is safely using. But, the effect of this locally applied agent on wound healing and inflammatory response was not evaluated adequately. Pedersen et al. suggested that with the prolonged use of lidocaineprilocaine cream, local inflammation and wound healing did not reduced.<sup>10</sup> In a small group of patients, who was performed dermabrassion with lidocaineprilocaine, Goodman concluded that the healing appeared to be faster and as good as traditional techniques.<sup>11</sup> But, Powell et al. did not recommended the use of lidocaineprilocaine cream in contaminated wounds.<sup>12</sup> They found that the cream elicited an exaggerated inflammatory response that damaged host defences, inviting the development of infection.

Lidocaine as a local analgesic agent was previously studied in clinic and experimental conditions. Local infiltration into the wound sites for pain relief was the main method, and it was found that pre- or intraoperative infiltration of lidocaine reduces pain, opioid consumption, and hospital stay after surgery.<sup>13,14</sup> In some experimental studies lidocaine did not alter the wound healing process,<sup>15,16</sup> but Morris and Tracey were found that intra- and subcutaneous injection of lidocaine had an adverse effect on wound healing at 5th and 7th day wounds.<sup>17</sup> For differentiating the effects of lidocaine and prilocaine, lidocaine cream was used in this study as a different treatment group.

We have seen that both of the treatment groups had better wound healing regarding to the control. Better tensile strength levels and higher 5-HP values in treatment groups supported these results. In an animal study with rats the effect of lidocaineprilocaine and 1% lidocaine infiltration on wound healing was studied, and it was found that lidocaineprilocaine cream does not affect wound healing adversely.<sup>18</sup> They found no wound infection or necrosis occurred in wounds and no significant difference between groups in the degree of inflammation. In wound healing models it is important to know the hydroxyproline content of the wounds because the tensile strength of a wound is strongly correlated with its hydroxyproline content. In this step the relation between lidocaineprilocaine cream application and the increased hydroxyproline production should be investigated. There is not enough literature data regarding to this issue. This increase may be related with the increased skin microvascular perfusion due to topical application of lidocaineprilocaine cream. After prolonged application greater than 3 hours of Ž . lidocaineprilocaine cream vasodilatation occurred in healthy subjects.<sup>19</sup> Arildsson et al. found that longer application time and higher intradermal concentration of the lidocaineprilocaine cream was associated with a delayed and persistent increase in the perfusion.<sup>20,21</sup> These may support the findings of the present study that lidocaineprilocaine cream does positively effect wound healing sequences.

In conclusion we may say that local anaesthetic agent lidocaineprilocaine cream has no controversial effect on experimental incisional wound healing model. Furthermore it may have some beneficial effects on wound healing which remains to be evaluated and it can be used safely in day-to-day emergency practices.
